# Gaddha — Art Direction

## Thesis

**Illustrated Indian civic realism:** a playable Indian newspaper cartoon with the human warmth and moral sincerity of *Swades*.

The game is warm and observant, never cynical about ordinary people. Humor may target broken systems and bureaucracy. Human consequences are treated sincerely.

## Visual balance

- 70% warm cinematic observation
- 20% editorial illustration
- 10% classical Indian compositional influence

Classical art should influence rhythm, framing, line, and storytelling—not appear as pasted ornamental borders.

## Palette

- Monsoon blue and indigo
- Weathered terracotta and road dust
- Bitumen charcoal
- Cotton white
- Muted neem green
- Rust red for danger
- Saffron or ochre only in small, purposeful moments

Never use fluorescent yellow, neon-on-black UI, glassmorphism, oversized marketing typography, ticker tape, dashboard cards, or excessive uppercase labels.

## Product hierarchy

1. The road
2. The pothole and repair action
3. The vehicle and person being protected
4. Weather and roadside life
5. Interface

The game world should fill the screen. Interface chrome stays quiet.

## Core loop

A simple vertical-scrolling road. A pothole and the vehicle it threatens travel downward, with the vehicle visibly gaining on the hazard. The player rubs back and forth over the pothole to complete three tactile stages:

1. Aggregate scatters into the cavity
2. Bitumen spreads across it
3. The surface compresses and settles

If completed in time, the vehicle passes smoothly and a human journey is saved. If incomplete, the tyre strikes, the suspension reacts, water and gravel jump, and the road loses health.

## Tone

The intended emotional response is: “That repair felt satisfying, but this problem is absurd and preventable.”

Saving a vehicle should create relief, not confetti. Failure should feel visceral, not slapstick.

## Mobile interaction direction — 2026-09-15

Karan's feedback: the game lacked a memorable identity, urgency, and reward. Prioritize iPhone/Safari. Rubbing should feel like doing work on the road; circling a progress indicator is not the desired gesture.

Use material displacement, friction-like sound, and a clear completion response as the baseline. Vibration is an optional enhancement, not a requirement. Keep a stationary hold alternative available for players who cannot comfortably rub.

Show cars and motorcycles as distinct road users. Motorcycle crashes can lead to a non-graphic loss or injury screen. State that the encounter is fictional and keep cited real-world figures separate from game rules. Do not present every pothole collision as fatal, invent a real victim, or imply that all reported pothole deaths were motorcyclists. Emphasize the human loss and the responsibility to maintain safe roads.
